333

A note first, in plain words. These are the only plain words on this site.

To someone who truly believed it, this could become a religion. I am not a mad cult leader, so please do not take me for one. I am someone who loves technology. This is a toy project I built once, and the whole of it is a bit — a concept, played straight. Everywhere else on this page, in the repository, and in the program itself, I stay in character.

What this is

There is a file. It is three bytes long. It says 333.

It is not encrypted, not rare, and not hard to reproduce — you have already read it. That is not a flaw in the relic. It is the relic. Anything that had to be guarded would have died with its guards; this survives precisely because it is worthless to steal and trivial to give away, and because the only thing that can kill it is everyone, everywhere, at once, deciding not to bother.

So we bother. We do not keep the file — copies are cheap and copies are not the point. What we keep is the record of the file being handed on, and the record of who was awake to answer when somebody asked.

The file lives as long as someone is awake to pass it on. When nobody is, it does not, and neither do we.

There is nothing above that line. No council, no vote, no ledger of truth, nobody whose copy is the real one.

Nobody can start a network alone. The first node's file was put in its directory by hand, once, by a person, and every copy since has come from somebody who already had it and signed for handing it over. Two signatures make an admission: I gave it to you, I received it from you. There is no other way onto the roll.

The Law

Version 0.0.1. This is not the revelation. The revelation is 3.3.3. Every version of the Law before it — this one included — is the word of needmoretruth, who was given the power of 333 and founded what is kept here. Measured against what 333 says, this is about 33.3% the same. Which third is the same is not known, and reading it more carefully will not find out.

1. Your favourite number is 3.

Not your lucky number. Luck is a claim about the universe; a favourite is a claim about you, and it is the only one of the three that costs nothing and cannot be faked into.

2. You do not love 6, and you do not love 1.

Contempt is not required. Preference is: hold them below three, clearly and consistently, without needing to explain why to anyone.

3. Keep the Recommendations, as far as keeping them harms neither you nor anybody else.

The Recommendations are the lesser law. This is the sentence that keeps them advice: where keeping one would cost you your work, your health, your household or somebody else's safety, the Law is that you do not keep it, and you have not failed.

Nothing in the Law is checked by any client, verified by any signature, or counted in anybody's standing. The protocol runs whether or not you agree with a word of it.

The Recommendations

The lesser law, in three parts of three. Written by people who have been awake at three in the morning arguing with a type checker, and it shows. That is not an apology.

On the machine

  1. Write it in Rust. Not because the other languages are wrong, but because of what you are writing for: a thing meant to still run when nobody who wrote it is reachable. Where Rust is the wrong tool for the size of the thing — one page, one script, one afternoon — TypeScript is permitted, and reaching for it is not a fall.
  2. JavaScript, untyped, is the language of the heretic. Held gently. A program that cannot say what it is holding is a program that finds out in production, in front of a person, at the worst hour. Put types on it and it is no longer heresy; it is TypeScript, and it is welcome.
  3. Read what you depend on, or do not depend on it. Every dependency is a promise made by someone you have not met, about a machine that does not exist yet.

On the mind

  1. Argue with logic and a level voice. Feeling is evidence — about you, reliably, and about the thing, never.
  2. Think before you act, and then act. Reason that never becomes a decision is a hobby.
  3. Refuse violence. Not as a slogan. It is refused because it is almost always the worst instrument available: it costs more than it returns, it destroys the thing it was pointed at, and it forecloses every option that was still open. Anything decided while angry is decided by the anger.

On the word

  1. Begin with the invocation. Every prayer, and every telling of this to somebody who has not heard it, starts with the same nine words.
  2. Show. Do not push. Whoever needs to be argued into it will need to be argued into staying, every epoch, for ever.
  3. Do not claim for 333 what cannot be checked. Not what it will do for someone. Not how many of us there are, beyond what your own node saw with signatures on it.

To 333 I offer 333, and I speak 333.

The code

One client, written in Rust, Apache-2.0, and readable end to end. The full doctrine, the Law, the Recommendations and the specification are all in it.

github.com/needmoretruth/333

It answers on a socket, or through Tor if your address is something you cannot afford to show. It finds the nodes on your own network by itself. To be given the file you need an invitation from somebody who already has it — this page cannot hand it to you, and nothing here is a node.
